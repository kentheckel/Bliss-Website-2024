00Benchmark · Summer 2026

Player culture won both. Only one team chose it.The Spurs took 86.6% of their Paris views out of 46% of their posts. The Lakers took 81.2% out of 81% — their posting mix, restated.

Three weeks apart, two NBA teams ran an overseas summer trip. On owned and athlete accounts the Lakers pulled 1.7× the views per post, and the Spurs converted their smaller audience harder. But the useful number is neither of those: divide each content bucket’s share of views by its share of posts and the two trips come apart. Paris over-indexed on player culture at 1.88×. Slovenia sat at 1.00× — a result you get for free when a bucket is most of what you posted.

The trade-off More views did not mean more reaction. On TikTok, the one surface where every post on both sides reports plays, the Spurs earned 183.6 engagements per 1,000 views to the Lakers’ 79.3 — Paris converting 2.3× harder across 8 and 12 posts. That is a ratio of sums, so it is a mean: drop each side’s single biggest TikTok and it becomes 163.9 against 77.4, or 2.1×. The direction holds; the size of the gap does not. The same rate cannot be computed across all posts at all, because Instagram carousels and statics book engagement with no plays, in unequal proportion on the two sides.
The anomaly Team accounts only: @spurs TikTok has 1.8M followers to @lakers’ 4.3M — 42% of the audience — and a median 19,074 engagements per post against 9,570 — 2.0× the rate, or 4.8× per follower. Read the median, not the mean: the Louvre slideshow alone is 69% of the Spurs’ entire Paris TikTok engagement, and on means it would carry the whole comparison.

          05Method & caveats

          Read this before quoting any of it.

          readme.txt–▢✕
          • Views are the least comparable number here. Instagram plays, TikTok plays and X impressions are three different counts. Instagram carousels and statics return nothing at all, so several high-engagement posts on both sides contribute zero views. Compare within a platform and treat cross-platform totals as directional.
          • Paris owned figures come from the Les Spurs / Spurs Give recap (post-level pull, 8/10/26): 28 posts, 5 accounts, 7/30–8/7. The single Facebook post is excluded because Slovenia has no Facebook equivalent. Paris TikTok view counts in that recap are rounded to the hundred, so any Paris rate quoted to a decimal inherits that rounding.
          • Slovenia owned figures come from a matching account-level pull of @lakers, @lukadoncic, @nba and @nbaeurope. 37 posts were hand-picked as trip content, posted 8/20–8/25, of which 32 survive the league-account exclusion. The pull returned 62 Lakers posts in that window; routine roster content, NBA Defense Week and a large Kobe Day block were set aside by hand as not trip content.
          • The two post sets were not assembled the same way, and that moves every per-post number. Paris’s 26 is a client-side judgement about what counted as trip content; Slovenia’s 32 is ours. The account mix is uneven too: Paris spans five accounts including athlete accounts such as @hbarnes, while Slovenia is @lakers 31 posts plus a single @lukadoncic post. Views per post and both index denominators depend on those counts, so read the index as a comparison of shape, not a precise multiple.
          • Slovenia’s content buckets were assigned by hand using the four categories from the Paris recap. Only two of them were ever needed. Bucket boundaries are a judgement call, and §02’s finding rests on them.
          • A handful of posts carry each total. The single biggest Paris post is 37% of all Paris owned views; the biggest Slovenia post is 15%. On earned X, one Bleacher Report post is roughly 45% of the entire Paris X total. Every multiple on this page is fragile to a few posts in both directions.
          • League accounts are excluded from both sides of every number on this page: @nbafrance from Paris, @nba and @nbaeurope from Slovenia. That includes the content mix and the top-post lists, so every post count on the page reconciles to 26 and 32.
          • Earned means nobody on the team posted it — media, fan and creator accounts only. Tottenham Hotspur transfer chatter was filtered out of the Paris set by hand; “Spurs” plus “Paris” catches a lot of football.
          • Engagement per 1,000 views is quoted on TikTok only. Every TikTok post on both sides reports plays. On Instagram, carousels and statics report engagement and no plays at all — 9 of the 20 Slovenia Instagram posts, and an unknown count in Paris — so an all-posts rate divides a full numerator by a partial denominator and inflates whichever side has more silent posts.
          • Index = share of views divided by share of posts. It is the only number here that separates “this worked” from “we posted a lot of this.” Paris player culture indexes at 1.88; Slovenia’s indexes at 1.00, meaning its 81% share of views is simply its 81% share of posts.
          • Both earned totals are floors, for different reasons. TikTok search is relevance-ranked and ignored the date bound, returning results back to 2021 — only 43 Paris TikTok posts landed in window against Slovenia’s 137, so Paris earned understates. Separately, the Slovenia sweep ran on Aug 24 and covers Aug 20 – Aug 24 against a trip that ran to Aug 25, so Slovenia understates on X and TikTok alike; Paris covers Jul 30 – Aug 8, its full window plus a day. Because the two shortfalls push opposite ways, no winner is marked on any earned view row except X, where both sides were queried identically and the shortfall can only widen the gap.
          • Engagement is likes + comments everywhere on this page, owned and earned alike. Shares and saves are TikTok-only, so they are left out of every cross-event number.
          • Instagram follower counts were not captured, so the per-follower normalisation is TikTok-only and team-account-only: @spurs (1.8M) against @lakers (4.3M).
          • Instagram earned was not pulled for either event on a like-for-like basis, so the earned comparison is X + TikTok only.
          • Not covered on either side: Stories, YouTube, paid-versus-organic split, follower delta, and sentiment.
